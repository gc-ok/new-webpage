The Build Loop
  1. Describe it. Explain the columns, the key numbers, and what the tool should show.
  2. Copy the code. AI writes the Apps Script. You don’t need to read it line by line.
  3. Paste it into Apps Script. One file for the logic, one for the page.
  4. Deploy. Publish it as a web app and choose who on your team can open it.

It’s a loop, not one prompt. Test it, tell the AI what to fix, paste the new code, and redeploy. Most tools take a few rounds.

Google Workspace is already the operational backbone for many schools.

FAQ

Questions districts ask before booking.

Do participants need coding experience?

No. These PD sessions are designed for administrators without a programming background. Participants learn to describe the workflow and outcome they need, then direct AI to write and revise the code with guided checkpoints along the way.

Our district uses Microsoft 365. Is this still a fit?

The hands-on builds use Google Workspace and Apps Script, which many schools already rely on. The planning skills—mapping a workflow, writing clear prompts, testing, and reviewing access—carry over to other platforms. Share your setup on the planning call and the session can be scoped to fit.

What happens to student data?

Hands-on exercises use synthetic or appropriately de-identified information. Sessions also cover access, human review, testing, accessibility, and local approval so tools built afterward follow your district's policies.

What do participants take home?

Depending on the format: starter prompts, planning templates, a responsible-use checklist, post-session resources, and for hands-on sessions, the prototype they built and tested.

What do participants need to bring?

Every hands-on participant should bring a laptop. Conference sessions are built around a live demonstration.

How is pricing set?

Pricing is provided after a short planning call so the scope reflects the audience, format, travel, and support required.
